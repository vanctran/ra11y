# 3.1.6 Pronunciation

**Level:** AAA | **Standard:** WCAG 2.2
**Spec:** <https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#pronunciation>

## Why manual

Detecting words with pronunciation-dependent meaning requires linguistic knowledge of the content domain. Static analysis cannot identify homographs, distinguish contexts where pronunciation disambiguates meaning, or verify that a pronunciation mechanism is present.

## What to verify

> "A mechanism is available to identify specific pronunciation information for words where meaning is ambiguous without knowing the pronunciation."

Applies to homographs and other words where meaning depends on pronunciation (e.g., "bass" fish vs. "bass" tone, "live" adjective vs. "live" verb, "read" present vs. "read" past tense). Mechanisms include: inline phonetic notation, a ruby annotation (`<ruby>`), an audio pronunciation guide, or a linked pronunciation key.

## Common failure modes

- Music content referring to "bass" without context or pronunciation cue.
- Technical documentation using "record" (noun) and "record" (verb) interchangeably without disambiguation.
- Multilingual content with loanwords whose pronunciation differs from the base language.
- Names of people, places, or products with non-obvious pronunciation affecting meaning (e.g., brand names).
- No pronunciation key or audio guide available for ambiguous terms.

## Attestation guidance

To attach evidence to an `attest` call (`ra11y-kb://criteria/wcag22/3.1.6`):

- Review content for words whose meaning changes based on pronunciation.
- Confirm each such word has a mechanism: phonetic text, `<ruby>` annotation, audio clip, or pronunciation guide link.
- Focus on domains where pronunciation ambiguity is common: music, linguistics, medicine (drug names), legal, multilingual.
- Verify the mechanism is accessible to screen readers.

Evidence: list of identified ambiguous words per page, mechanism provided for each.
